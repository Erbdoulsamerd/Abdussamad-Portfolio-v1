# About Me

# Abdussamad Ibrahim

**Creative Systems Designer**

---

## Who I Am

I am Abdussamad Ibrahim, also known as Flint—a multidisciplinary designer based in Nigeria whose practice exists at the intersection of identity, culture, objects, spaces, and experiences.

Although I began my journey through brand identity design, my work has continually expanded beyond traditional boundaries. Today, I work across branding, creative direction, industrial design, photography, editorial design, spatial thinking, hospitality concepts, and community building. I don't see these as separate disciplines, but as different ways of expressing the same idea.

For me, design is not defined by the medium. The medium simply becomes the most appropriate tool for solving a problem.

---

## My Philosophy

I believe the strongest ideas are not confined to one discipline.

A visual identity can shape perception.
A product can encourage interaction.
A space can influence behavior.
A photograph can preserve culture.
A community can change lives.

Rather than creating isolated deliverables, I design systems that connect people, places, ideas, and experiences.

Every project starts with curiosity and ends with one question:

**How can this become more meaningful?**

---

## My Practice

My work moves fluidly between the digital and physical world.

I have designed brand identities for businesses, startups, and cultural organizations. I have built physical structures like a skateboard ramp to support a growing skateboarding community. I have developed hospitality concepts where branding, architecture, interiors, and storytelling become one experience. Through photography, I document people, places, and ideas with the same intentionality that I bring to design.

Projects such as Flint, SkateMania, Gidan Kasa, The Backyard, and numerous identity systems all represent different expressions of a single practice—designing experiences that people can participate in rather than simply observe.

---

## What Drives Me

I am fascinated by the relationship between design and culture.

I enjoy understanding how people move, gather, communicate, remember, and create meaning. Whether I am developing a visual identity, directing a photoshoot, prototyping an object, or imagining a new public space, my goal remains the same: to create work that feels intentional, useful, and lasting.

Growing up in Northern Nigeria has significantly influenced my perspective. It has inspired me to explore local stories, architecture, youth culture, craftsmanship, and community through a contemporary design lens.

## Looking Forward

My ambition is to continue building projects that blur the boundaries between disciplines.

I want to create brands that become communities, spaces that become destinations, products that become rituals, and ideas that leave a lasting cultural impact.

I don't believe great design lives in one category.

I believe it lives in the connections between them.
